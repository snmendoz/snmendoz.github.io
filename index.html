01 / about

About

I work at the intersection of metabolic engineering and systems biology — a field usually called metabolic systems engineering. My research builds and applies mathematical models of cellular metabolism to understand how organisms allocate resources, and to design better ways of producing high-value compounds in cell factories.

Advances in sequencing and in the mass quantification of transcripts, proteins and metabolites have made omics data abundant and cheap. Integrating that data into metabolic models produces markedly more accurate predictions of cellular behaviour — and that integration is where most of my work sits: metabolic network reconstruction, omics integration, modeling of microbial communities, artificial intelligence for model improvement, and advanced optimization of metabolic networks.

I did my Ph.D. in systems biology at the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, in Bas Teusink's Systems Biology Lab, on a Becas Chile fellowship. Before and since, I have worked on both sides of the academic–industrial boundary: as Chief Operating Officer at STICTA Biologicals leading the bioprocess area, as an advisor to Chr. Hansen on yogurt fermentation, and as a project engineer building models for wastewater treatment plants. That range is deliberate — the methods travel well across microbes, algae, fungi and mammalian cells.

I am currently a scientist at the Center for Mathematical Modeling at Universidad de Chile, and available to supervise undergraduate and graduate thesis students.

Research lines

  • iiMicrobial community modelingDetermining interspecies interactions, nutritional requirements and minimal synthetic consortia.
  • iiiOmics data integrationConstraining models with transcriptomic, proteomic and metabolomic measurements for context-specific predictions.
  • ivArtificial intelligence for metabolic modelsPredicting enzymatic parameters and coupling learned models with classical stoichiometric and kinetic methods.
  • vAdvanced optimization for strain and bioprocess designMulti-level and mixed-integer linear programming applied to cell factories and fermentation processes.

Current project — microbial communities along the coast of Chile

Thanks to the CEODOS Mission Microbiome expedition, more than 150 metagenomes and metatranscriptomes are available along the Chilean coast, from Iquique to Punta Arenas. From that genetic content, this project reconstructs metabolic networks to determine the metabolic potential of the microbial community in each sample, and so identify the characteristics that define each maritime zone of the country.

The work brings together metabolic reconstruction, integration of metatranscriptomic data, community modeling for interspecies interactions, artificial intelligence to predict enzymatic parameters, and advanced optimization to determine minimal synthetic marine communities. The results are intended as input for public policy aimed at protecting key ecosystem zones along the coast, as well as for improving economic productivity.

Selected work

  • A systematic assessment of current genome-scale metabolic reconstruction tools Genome Biology · 2019 · first author

    A first-authored comparison of the tools available for reconstructing genome-scale metabolic models, with practical guidelines for users. Over 100 citations.

  • Creation and analysis of biochemical constraint-based models using the COBRA Toolbox v.3.0 Nature Protocols · 2019 · over 800 citations

    The current standard protocol for constraint-based analysis and reconstruction of metabolic networks. My contribution was software development for metabolic engineering and strain design.

  • Yeast9: a consensus genome-scale metabolic model for S. cerevisiae curated by the community Molecular Systems Biology · 2024

    The current version of the consensus yeast metabolic reconstruction, already cited more than 60 times. My contribution was network curation, particularly of annotations.

  • A comprehensive genome-scale metabolic model for porcine cells for the cultivated meat industry Future Foods · 2026 · corresponding author

    A model for a mammalian cell type, with applications to process optimization and design. I conceived the investigation and led funding acquisition, formal analysis, writing and supervision.

  • Reconstruction of the microalga Nannochloropsis salina genome-scale metabolic model with applications to lipid production BMC Systems Biology · 2017

    Shows that metabolic models can propose metabolic engineering strategies that improve titers and yields of high-value compounds. My contribution was building the model.
